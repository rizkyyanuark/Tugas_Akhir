\begin{Abstrak}

    Pencarian literatur di repositori institusi masih sering terganjal oleh keterbatasan mendasar, yaitu sistem berbasis kata kunci tidak menangkap makna, sedangkan \textit{Retrieval-Augmented Generation} (RAG) konvensional berbasis vektor cenderung kehilangan konteks hubungan antar-dokumen. Penelitian ini membangun sistem pencarian akademik yang memanfaatkan \textit{Knowledge Graph} sebagai tulang punggung konteks, dengan arsitektur \textit{dual-indexing} yang mengintegrasikan \textit{vector database} dan \textit{graph database}. Dari data publikasi dosen rumpun Infokom UNESA, \textit{pipeline} yang dikembangkan berhasil menyusun graf heterogen berisi 1.253 simpul dan 1.970 relasi secara otomatis. Untuk mengukur kualitas jawaban, digunakan paradigma \textit{LLM-as-a-Judge} secara berpasangan (\textit{pairwise}) terhadap tiga mode pencarian, yaitu \textit{Naive} (vektor murni), \textit{Subgraph} (navigasi graf lokal), dan \textit{Hybrid} (gabungan keduanya). Dari 336 skenario perbandingan, mode \textit{Subgraph} justru tampil paling konsisten dengan \textit{win rate} keseluruhan 48,7\%, lebih tinggi dibanding \textit{Hybrid}. Temuan yang agak mengejutkan adalah mode \textit{Hybrid}, meski menyediakan konteks paling kaya, malah memperburuk metrik keterlacakan (\textit{Traceability}) hingga 44,2\% akibat kelebihan informasi yang membuat model bahasa kehilangan fokus. Hasil ini menunjukkan bahwa dalam domain akademik, menyuntikkan konteks graf yang terarah dan ringkas lebih berguna daripada membanjiri model dengan seluruh informasi relasional yang tersedia.

    \katakunci{\textit{Knowledge Graph, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, GraphRAG, Dual-Indexing, LLM-as-a-Judge}}
\end{Abstrak}

\begin{Abstract}

    Literature search in institutional repositories remains constrained by a fundamental gap. Keyword-based systems miss semantic meaning, while conventional vector-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) tends to lose inter-document relational context. This study builds an academic search system that leverages a Knowledge Graph as its contextual backbone, using a dual-indexing architecture that integrates a vector database and a graph database. From publication data of Infokom faculty members at UNESA, the developed pipeline automatically constructed a heterogeneous graph containing 1,253 nodes and 1,970 relationships. Answer quality was measured through a pairwise LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation across three retrieval modes, namely Naive (pure vector), Subgraph (local graph navigation), and Hybrid (combined). Across 336 comparison scenarios, the Subgraph mode proved the most consistent, achieving an overall win rate of 48.7\%, higher than Hybrid. A somewhat surprising finding was that the Hybrid mode, despite providing the richest context, actually degraded Traceability to 44.2\% due to information overload that caused the language model to lose focus. These results suggest that in the academic domain, injecting targeted and concise graph context is more useful than flooding the model with all available relational information.

    \keywords{Knowledge Graph, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, GraphRAG, Dual-Indexing, LLM-as-a-Judge}
\end{Abstract}
